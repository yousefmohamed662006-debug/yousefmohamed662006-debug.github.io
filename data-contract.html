Data quality case study

Defining what the warehouse data actually means.

Before modeling, I documented the unit, time window, field meanings, missingness, verification queries, approved features, and a leakage trap for the FlyRank search-intelligence release.

Why the contract mattered

A clean-looking table can still produce a wrong model when the row meaning, time window, or source coverage is misunderstood. The contract converted a large warehouse release into a shared set of assumptions that could be checked before feature engineering.

Core definitions

  • Unit: one content page for one reporting month and client.
  • Example verification month: March 2026.
  • Source families: Google Search Console, GA4, traffic-channel sessions, and AI-referral fields.
  • Coverage flags: fields such as client_has_gsc and client_has_ga4 indicate whether zeros can be interpreted safely.

Representative fields

Search visibility

gsc_impressions, gsc_clicks, gsc_sum_position, and gsc_avg_position.

Engagement

ga4_pageviews, ga4_sessions, ga4_users, ga4_engaged_sessions, and total engagement seconds.

Traffic mix

Organic, direct, referral, social, paid, and AI-attributed sessions.

AI sources

ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, and Meta referral indicators.

Verification plan

I designed checks to confirm the release before modeling:

  1. Confirm the expected reporting month and row grain.
  2. Check duplicate page-client-month combinations.
  3. Compare null and zero patterns against the GSC and GA4 availability flags.
  4. Inspect whether derived rates use valid denominators.
  5. Verify that fields selected for modeling are available at recommendation time.

Feature and leakage decisions

Candidate features were selected from information available before the decision, such as page age, search visibility, traffic volume, engagement, and channel mix. Label-derived fields, future windows, identifiers, and review-only context were excluded from model inputs.

Trap documented: a field can look predictive because it was calculated after the outcome window or built from the label. High correlation is not enough; availability timing must be verified.

Outcome

The result was not a model score. It was a safer modeling foundation: explicit assumptions, reproducible checks, and a record of which fields could be trusted. That contract later supported the baseline and Logistic Regression work without silently changing the meaning of the data.
