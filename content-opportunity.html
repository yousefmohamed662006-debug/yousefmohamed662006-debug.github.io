Content Opportunity Scoring: Ranking Web Pages for Refresh Review Under Grouped Client Holdout Validation

An evidence-grounded machine learning study on 30,000 anonymized multi-client pages, evaluating hand-crafted heuristics against leakage-safe ranking pipelines.

Abstract

Content editorial teams manage large digital libraries but possess constrained manual review bandwidth, making exhaustive page-by-page audits operationally infeasible. In this study, we frame content refresh prioritization as a ranking problem across 30,000 anonymized web pages spanning 32 client organizations. To prevent catastrophic feature leakage between multi-page client domains, we employ a strict grouped client-holdout split (24 training clients, 8 testing clients). Our learned Logistic Regression ranking model achieves a held-out Precision@50 of 0.74 (37 true decline opportunities in the top 50 recommendations), outperforming the baseline heuristic rule of 0.36 by +105%. These results demonstrate that leakage-safe machine learning ranking substantially improves review queue quality while providing transparent operational reason codes for domain editors.

1. Introduction & Problem Statement

Web content decay is a critical operational bottleneck in modern digital publishing. Pages gradually lose organic traffic and search visibility due to outdated information, shifting search intent, and competitive displacement. However, reviewing every underperforming URL is costly. The actionable business requirement is not a binary classification of all historical pages, but a ranked recommendation queue where top-ranked items yield maximum editorial ROI.

2. Dataset & Data Hygiene

The study utilizes the anonymized FlyRank ML Internship dataset release consisting of 30,000 rows across 45 structured search and content signals.

  • Public Safety & Privacy: In compliance with data-use governance, all URLs, client names, search queries, and proprietary domain identifiers were fully stripped.
  • Temporal & Structural Windows: Signals include 90-day aggregate impressions, historical click-through rates (CTR), days since last update, and search position velocity.

3. Methodology & Leakage-Safe Validation

A major vulnerability in multi-client SEO data is cross-client domain leakage: random train/test splitting distributes URLs from the same client across both sets, enabling the model to memorize client-specific baselines rather than generalizable decay patterns.

Validation Design: We enforced a strict Grouped Client Split (24 clients / 22,885 rows in train; 8 clients / 7,115 rows in test with 0% client overlap). Preprocessing scalers and imputers were fitted exclusively on the training partition.

The baseline was established using a standard heuristic filter selecting pages with low historical CTR (< 0.07) and substantial search impressions (> 731). We compared this against a calibrated Logistic Regression ranker and a Hybrid rule-guarded ranker.

4. Experimental Results

Performance was evaluated primarily on Precision@K (the proportion of genuinely declining pages in the top K recommendations), reflecting true operational value:

5. Limitations & Decision-Support Framing

In accordance with research integrity, this model is framed strictly as an observed decision-support tool. The proxy label (is_declining_label) indicates statistical decay trends, not causal proof that an editorial rewrite will guarantee rank recovery. Content managers must combine algorithmic scores with domain expertise.
